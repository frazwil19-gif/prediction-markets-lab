## 1. What actually happened (both runs)

5,776 of 5,800 Cycle 1 matches were eligible and included (24 excluded as `eligible_consensus_model=False`, a pre-existing Cycle 1 flag, not something this backtest created). Every included match produced 3 candidates (home/draw/away) through the exact production pipeline (`probability.market_pipeline` → `decisions.recommendation.build_recommendation`), for 17,328 candidates total, in both the closing-snapshot (primary) and opening-snapshot (secondary) runs.

**Headline result: zero money-qualified bets in both runs.** `money_decision` was `REJECT` for 15,993/17,328 candidates (92.3%, closing) — the underlying research grade itself was never actionable (C or Reject) — and `PAPER_ONLY` for the remaining 1,335 (7.7%) — an actionable A/B research grade that still failed one or more money-qualification gates. Zero candidates reached `BET`. Grade `A+` never occurred in either run (grade counts, closing: Reject 14,297, C 1,696, B 1,328, A 7, A+ 0).

**This is a real, honestly-obtained negative result, not a bug.** It was independently reproduced under both price-timing snapshots (0/17,328 qualified with closing odds, 0/17,328 with opening odds), so it is not an artefact of the specific closing-snapshot proxy design choice.

## 2. Why: a specific, mechanically explainable structural finding

Every one of the 1,335 actionable-grade `PAPER_ONLY` candidates was inspected. The pattern is uniform and mechanically clear:

- **All 269 "High"-confidence actionable candidates have an estimated probability below 50%** — zero exceptions. The single highest was 45.6% (grade B, net EV +3.0%, rejected solely for probability).
- Of the 1,064 "Medium"-confidence actionable candidates, 1,054 also sit below 50% probability (and therefore below even the *base* 50% floor, let alone Medium's stricter 60% floor).
- **The single closest miss across all 17,328 candidates failed by 0.01 percentage points of net EV**: match `6c143d2f6763e606`, selection "away," 68.6% probability, odds 1.53, Medium confidence — net EV was 4.99%, one basis point under the 5.00% Medium-confidence value floor. Every other requirement (probability 68.6% > 60% floor, odds 1.53 inside the preferred 1.40-2.50 band) was already satisfied.

**The mechanism**: in this market design, a candidate's positive net EV comes from one bookmaker's price disagreeing favourably with the cross-bookmaker consensus (an EV signal built entirely from bookmaker dispersion, since there is no independent probability model feeding this comparison — see the feasibility audit §2.2, production V1's probability *is* the consensus). Cross-bookmaker disagreement is systematically larger, in relative odds terms, on the less-fancied side of a match (a small absolute probability disagreement translates into a much larger relative-odds gap at long prices than at short ones) — the classic favourite-longshot-bias-adjacent effect. The practical consequence: **the "positive EV via bookmaker disagreement" signal and the "probability floor" requirement pull in opposite directions for almost every candidate this data source can produce.** This is precisely the failure mode `decisions/money_qualification.py`'s own docstring was written to prevent (the Ipswich-away-at-14.00 and Leeds-away-at-10.00 examples that motivated the gate) — this backtest is direct historical evidence that the gate, as configured, does exactly what it was designed to do: it is correctly rejecting the dominant pattern of "apparent edge" this consensus-vs-consensus-best-price design produces, at the cost of also rejecting a small number of near-miss candidates like the 68.6%/net-EV-4.99% one above that a person might reasonably judge as an acceptable bet.

## 3. Probability quality: a genuinely positive, separate finding

Independently of the zero-money-qualified result, the underlying probability estimate (the bookmaker consensus itself) is well calibrated against real historical outcomes across all 17,328 candidates: mean estimated probability 33.33% vs. actual outcome rate 33.33%, Brier score 0.196, log loss 0.576, expected calibration error 0.0099, calibration slope 1.07 (very close to the ideal of 1.0 — very slightly overconfident), AUC 0.696. This matches Gate 1's own prior finding that the raw consensus is a strong, well-calibrated baseline (`gate1_1x2_architecture_results.json`) and is not a new discovery, but it is useful corroboration from an independent metric implementation (`performance.binary_classification`, not Gate 1's own scoring code) on the full candidate population rather than a filtered subset. **This finding does not depend on the zero-money-qualified result and should not be read as contradicting it** — a well-calibrated probability source can still, by construction of a *different* EV-generation mechanism (bookmaker disagreement, not the consensus itself), produce a candidate pool whose apparent "edge" cases skew toward the wrong side of a probability floor.

## 4. Conservative checklist (Section 21 of the governing instruction)

- **Exact or proxy, and why**: PROXY. Same-snapshot bookmaker odds used for both sides of the comparison (no timestamp leakage — see the leakage audit); the simulated scan timestamp is a fixed 60-minute-before-kickoff construct, not a real historical fact, which structurally prevents this run from testing the money-event-horizon gate at all (feasibility audit §5).
- **Coverage**: 5,776/5,800 matches (99.6%), 3 competitions, 6 seasons, both price snapshots. Zero matches excluded during replay itself (0 excluded_missing_complete_bookmaker_panel, 0 excluded_kickoff_join_failed) — the 24 exclusions are entirely upstream, pre-existing Cycle 1 flags.
- **Number of money-qualified bets**: 0 (both runs). Sample size is not an issue here in the usual sense — there is no small-sample-size risk to a "zero" result; it is a clean, fully-explained structural zero, not a truncated small sample.
- **Calibration quality**: strong (ECE 0.0099, slope 1.07) — see §3.
- **Made or lost money**: neither — no bet was ever placed, so there is no P&L, ROI, drawdown, or losing streak to report (all reported as explicit "no bets" rather than zero-by-omission — see each run's `performance.json`, `betting_performance.overall.note`).
- **Sample size adequate or noisy**: N/A for betting performance (no bets); the 17,328-candidate probability-quality sample is large and not noisy.
- **Historical vs. live-paper comparability**: not yet comparable — live paper trading only began 2026-09-20 and has not yet accumulated enough history for its own comparison; this is a known, stated gap, not glossed over.
- **Leakage/implementation-bug evidence**: none found. The leakage audit's verification tests (chronological ordering, settlement-after-recommendation, price-timing never mixed, best-price-from-accepted-bookmakers-only) all pass (`tests/unit/test_backtest_replay.py`, `test_backtest_historical_loader.py`).
- **Justified vs. not-justified conclusions**: Justified — "this specific EV-generation mechanism (bookmaker-disagreement-vs-consensus, no independent probability model) rarely if ever produces a candidate that also clears a real-money probability floor, in this historical sample." **Not justified** — "the money-qualification gate is too strict," "the probability floor should be lowered," "this proves V1 will never place a real bet." None of these follow from one proxy backtest on one data source, and none are recommended here (Section 20 of the governing instruction forbids any threshold change in this phase regardless).

## 5. Forward chronological development/validation protocol design (not executed — Section 12)

For any FUTURE phase that does propose threshold or model changes (never in Phase 1), the following protocol is designed now, in advance, precisely so it cannot later be bent to fit whatever a specific result showed (`docs/DATA_LEAKAGE_RULES.md` rule 8):

1. **Split unit**: whole seasons, not individual matches or dates (matches `validation.time_splits.WalkForwardFold`'s existing season-label convention, already used by Gate 1 and Stage 3B).
2. **Proposed split** (to be fixed and hash-sealed *before* any development work begins, not after): development seasons `2020_21`-`2023_24` (4 seasons, ~3,800 candidates at the current inclusion rate), validation season `2024_25` (~950 candidates), sealed holdout season `2025_26` (~950 candidates, already partially the subject of the existing `h_fb2_002_sealed_oos_2025_26_*` files from prior Cycle 2 research — a future phase must decide explicitly whether to reuse that exact seal or draw a fresh one, and record which, rather than silently assuming continuity).
3. **What may be tuned in development**: money-qualification thresholds only (probability floor, EV floor, confidence eligibility, event-horizon hours) — never the underlying consensus/grading logic itself in the same pass, to keep each phase's changes attributable.
4. **What must never be tuned on validation or holdout data**: anything. Per `validation.leakage_checks.check_no_test_period_in_development`, any development-phase touch of validation/holdout dates invalidates that season for its intended purpose from then on — this must be enforced by literally calling that function against the actual date/season boundaries a future run uses, not merely asserted in prose.
5. **Explicit statement now, before any development happens**: given the zero-money-qualified finding in §1-2, a genuinely useful next research question (for a FUTURE phase, not this one) is not "which threshold value produces more bets" but "does an independent probability signal (Elo/Poisson/blend — already researched in Cycle 2/Gate 1, but never fed into the money-qualification EV comparison) produce positive-EV candidates on the FAVOURITE side as well as the underdog side, changing the shape of §2's structural finding." That is a probability-architecture question, not a threshold-tuning question, and would require its own new leakage audit (rolling-feature leakage, per `docs/DATA_LEAKAGE_RULES.md` rules 2 and 5, which this Phase 1 backtest never needed to check because V1's probability is the raw consensus with no rolling state).
6. **Small-sample honesty commitment**: if a future validation run produces fewer than ~50 money-qualified bets (a plausible outcome given §1-2's structural finding, even under a modified strategy), any ROI/win-rate figure from it must be reported with its bootstrap CI foregrounded, not the point estimate alone — this is not a new rule, it restates `backtesting.metrics`'s existing purpose, extended forward to whatever produces that future run.

## 6. Data sufficiency verdict (Section 13)

Restating and finalising `BACKTEST_DATA_FEASIBILITY_AUDIT.md` §6 in light of the actual run:

**Sufficient, and used**: Cycle 1's 1X2 dataset was sufficient to run a real, honest, fully-reasoned frozen-V1 proxy backtest end-to-end, producing a genuine (if null) result and a mechanically explained reason for it — this is exactly what Phase 1 asked for ("run the first frozen-V1 backtest only where the data supports it"), and it was possible without acquiring any new data.

**Not sufficient, and not purchased or recommended for purchase**: (a) Over/Under 2.5 and Asian Handicap remain unusable for a money-qualification backtest under current thresholds (2-3 bookmakers per match, below the 3-bookmaker data-quality floor) — more free or paid bookmaker panels for these markets would help, but nothing here recommends buying one merely because it exists; the existing free 1X2 data already answered Phase 1's core question. (b) A genuine test of the money-event-horizon gate needs real intraday multi-timestamp price data, which no historical free source in this repository provides — the live daily scans running since 2026-09-20 are the actual, already-in-progress fix for this, given enough elapsed calendar time (months, not this session), not a data-purchase decision. (c) True exchange execution CLV remains unavailable per the pre-existing, unchanged `docs/HISTORICAL_PRICE_AND_CLV_LIMITATIONS.md` restriction — unaffected by anything in Phase 1.

**Bottom line**: no data purchase is recommended. The next informative step, when a future phase is authorized to take it, is a probability-architecture question (§5.5), not a data-acquisition question.
